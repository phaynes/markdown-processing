# Automating The Preparation of Publication Grade Technical Documentation

## TLDR
### Problem: Disconnection of Published Documentation and Source Technical Material

The preparation of publication-grade technical documents, such as commercial bids, formal software project documents, cyber security reports, and research papers, typically begins with the collation and preparation of material obtained from various technical environments and collaborators. This might include research papers, designs, requirements, security controls, code, and Wiki material.

At some stage, this material is copied to a separate publication environment to prepare the documentation for submission. Instead of leveraging the collaborative tooling and automation available in modern software development environments, efforts are redirected towards the direct final work product using office tools such as Word and Excel. This often involves intricate and tightly controlled manual processes. Investments made in producing the final artefacts are rarely rolled back into the source material, causing the documentation and underlying work products to remain disconnected.

This disconnection is problematic for several reasons:

1. **Complexity and Error-Prone Processes:** The creation and handling of the final artefact is often highly intricate and prone to errors, whether it is an APA7 academic paper, a security audit report, or a commercial submission. The ability to automatically generate a final submission from source material, subject to auditable change control, allows for economies of scale across submissions, automated validation, and reliable delivery as deadlines approach.

2. **High Costs:** Updates to production deliverables often incur high costs, typically requiring the involvement of key technical personnel due to the stakes involved.

3. **Challenges in Automated Cross-Checking:** Automated cross-checking of material across sources becomes challenging or impossible. This includes tracing requirements to designs/test cases, in-text references to bibliography, and security controls to design and implementation, all to continuously produce a final security authority to operate (ATO) report.

4. **Misunderstandings and Errors:** Teams who originated the source material may now be required to produce what was finally agreed upon, leading to potential misunderstandings and errors due to scope drift.

### Why is this Problem a Problem

In practice, and not unsurprisingly, the production of publication-grade documents is not performed by technical people, but rather by individuals for whom writing and communication are their primary skills. Job roles might include marketers, lawyers, commercial directors, project directors, and sales managers.

These individuals use the tools and technologies they are familiar with, rather than those more aligned with the overall project outcomes.

Conversely, within technical environments, the tools required to perform basic functions such as spelling and grammar checking are often unavailable. The format of source material is complicated, perhaps embedded in code comments or JSON/XML documents, making proofing difficult. Additionally, high-quality writing is not always the strongest skill of the technical team members.

Finally, there is the fundamental technical practicality of a mechanism to transform source material into a final publication-grade work product.

### Solving the Publication Grade Documentation challenge










This project aims to reduce the preparation time of publication-grade, by 25-50% through an integrated set of automation technologies. The technologies developed for this purpose include:

1. Submission generation from markdown text that includes full APAv7 referencing and style requirements.
2. Highly customisable proofing, reviewing, and commenting on documents using multiple AIs, either directly within an IDE (Zed), from the command line, or as part of a CI/CD process. Larger changes are controlled through git.
3. Managing collected references using AI summarisation and search.
4. Securely collaborating in real-time with other team members using Zed and mesh networking.
5. APAv7 styled markdown document viewers.

Data is managed using either public or private git repositories.

## Introduction

The preparation of high-quality research papers is complex, involving two kinds of difficulty:

1. **Inherent difficulty:** The subject being studied, including its key ideas, nuances, and issues.
2. **Accidental difficulty:** The extensive literature, understanding the overall issues in the field, synthesising information to answer research questions, preparing a paper to high standards, ensuring completely accurate referencing according to academic standards like APAv7, and doing this in a timely manner. This process is often particularly challenging for individuals from technical fields.

By leveraging emerging technologies, this project aims to foster the development and distribution of automated methods for preparing research papers, thus alleviating the accidental complexities typically associated with this process. Researchers can then focus their available time on the subject of interest or present insights with the greatest clarity, without the time drain usually associated with preparing publication-grade materials.

Acknowledging that the topic of preparation itself is a new field, the author welcomes feedback.

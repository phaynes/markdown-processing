**Summary (in relation to: "What is the optimal application of explicit teaching to high school science classroom practices to improve learning outcomes for students with autism spectrum disorder (ASD)"):**
The text describes experiences of neurodivergent students, including those with autism spectrum disorder (ASD), in graduate STEM programs, highlighting challenges and potential strengths these students bring to the field. In terms of high school science classroom practices, explicit teaching methods could optimally improve learning outcomes for students with ASD by providing clear, structured guidance that addresses their unique executive function impairments, such as difficulties with working memory, goal setting, and self-regulation. This approach should encompass strategies to support organizing ideas, maintaining focus, and initiating tasks, tailored to the individual needs of autistic students. Additionally, fostering an inclusive environment that appreciates neurodiversity could empower these students to leverage their strengths, like pattern recognition and systemizing skills, thereby enhancing their engagement and success in science education.

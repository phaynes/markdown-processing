**Summary (in relation to: "What is the optimal application of explicit teaching to high school science classroom practices to improve learning outcomes for students with autism spectrum disorder (ASD)"):**
The meta-analysis titled "Written Expression in Individuals with Autism Spectrum Disorder" highlights challenges and techniques in written expression for students with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), providing valuable insights for improving high school science classroom practices. To optimize learning outcomes for students with ASD, explicit teaching should address the unique difficulties these students face in written expression—such as issues with handwriting, spelling, and sentence construction—by employing targeted interventions. Here are key strategies derived from the analysis:

1. **Explicit Instruction in Mechanics:** Given that students with ASD may struggle with the mechanics of writing due to motor planning or sensory integration issues, explicit teaching in handwriting, spelling, and sentence structure is crucial. Emphasizing practice in these areas can help alleviate the cognitive load associated with mechanical aspects of writing.

2. **Use of Digital Tools:** Incorporating technology, such as spell-check or typing software, can support students by providing alternatives to traditional handwriting and allowing them more cognitive space to focus on scientific content and the organization of their ideas.

3. **Phonological and Morphological Training:** Since spelling is a common area of difficulty and involves phonological, orthographic, and morphological processing, structured teaching addressing these components can strengthen transcription skills. This approach leverages the relative strength in rote learning observed in some individuals with ASD.

4. **Syntax and Sentence Construction:** Teaching should include explicit instruction in constructing sentences, understanding syntax, and selecting appropriate vocabulary. This can be facilitated by breaking down complex ideas into manageable components, making it easier for students with ASD to express scientific concepts clearly.

5. **Structured Writing Processes:** High school science teachers should emphasize a structured writing process that involves planning, drafting, revising, and editing. The use of graphic organizers, outlines, and visual aids can support students with ASD in organizing their thoughts coherently.

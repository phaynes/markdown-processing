**Summary (in relation to: "What is the optimal application of explicit teaching to high school science classroom practices to improve learning outcomes for students with autism spectrum disorder (ASD)"):**
The study focuses on improving writing skills for high school students with ADHD using the Self-Regulated Strategy Development (SRSD) model. While it is not directly about teaching strategies for students with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) in high school science classes, it provides insights into how explicit teaching strategies, such as SRSD, can enhance learning outcomes for students with specific educational needs.

For optimizing teaching strategies for students with ASD, consider the following adaptations based on the current study’s framework:

1. **Explicit Instruction**: Clearly teach specific strategies for learning and organizing information, similar to how writing strategies are explicitly taught using SRSD. This can help students with ASD to better organize their thoughts and approach problem-solving effectively in science contexts.

2. **Scaffolding and Support**: Just as SRSD reduces working memory demands, science teachers can use visual aids, structured templates, and cues to support students with ASD, helping to manage cognitive load and improve understanding.

3. **Self-Regulation and Goal-Setting**: Integrating self-regulation strategies and clear goal-setting, as seen in SRSD, can empower students with ASD to monitor their progress, maintain focus, and achieve academic goals in science subjects.

4. **Individualized Instruction**: The one-on-one scenario in the study underscores the importance of personalized support. Similarly, science educators could provide personalized or small group instruction to address the unique learning needs of students with ASD.

5. **Regular Feedback and Monitoring**: Continuous feedback and monitoring, akin to the study’s method of tracking writing improvements, can be adapted to track students’ understanding and application of science concepts, facilitating ongoing adjustments to teaching strategies.

Overall, applying the structured and strategic approach from this study could improve science learning outcomes for high school students with ASD by focusing on explicit instruction, scaffolding, self-regulation, individualized support, and consistent feedback.
